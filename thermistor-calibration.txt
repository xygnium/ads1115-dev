Yes, you can absolutely use Sodium Sulfate Anhydrous (ACS Grade), but you cannot use it straight out of the bottle as a dry powder. Because the 32.38°C fixed point is a hydrate transition, you must first add a precise amount of distilled water to hydrate it into a saturated slush.The ACS Reagent Grade classification is perfect for this, as its high purity (≥99.0%) ensures your plateau will hit exactly 32.374°C to 32.383°C without any impurity-induced temperature depression.The Hydration MathAnhydrous sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄) has a molar mass of 142.04 g/mol, while the decahydrate form (Na₂SO₄ ⋅ 10H₂O) has a molar mass of 322.20 g/mol. The crystal lattice requires exactly 55.9% water by weight just to build the hydrate.To have excess liquid water available to form the necessary liquid/solid calibration slush, you want to overshoot the hydration threshold slightly. A highly reliable ratio for preparing a calibration standard from anhydrous powder is:70 grams of ACS Anhydrous Sodium Sulfate100 grams (mL) of distilled waterStep-by-Step Preparation ProtocolTo turn your dry ACS powder into a high-precision 32.38°C calibration standard:Dissolve and Heat: Combine the anhydrous powder and distilled water in a clean glass beaker. Heat the mixture on a stove or hotplate to roughly 40°C to 45°C, stirring thoroughly until the powder completely dissolves into a clear, hot, saturated solution.Cool to Initiate Crystallization: Remove the beaker from the heat source and allow it to cool down toward room temperature while stirring constantly. As the liquid drops past 32.38°C, large, translucent decahydrate crystals will rapidly precipitate out.Capture the Phase Transition: The beaker will quickly turn into a thick, snowy, wet salt-slush. Because the transition is highly exothermic, the temperature of this mix will lock stubbornly at 32.38°C as the crystals and liquid equilibrate.Insert the Sensor: Submerge your sealed thermistor probe directly into the thickest part of the slush, keep stirring the mixture gently around the sensor, and take your resistance reading once the electrical values stabilize.Storage TipOnce you are done, you do not have to discard the mix. Seal the slush in an airtight jar.
